# Evidence base

Only needed if the learner asks why the skill works this way, or when maintaining the skill. Keep explanations to the learner short; one mechanism and one study is usually enough.

| Mechanism | Methods in this skill | Key evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Illusion of understanding | Reason the skill checks by production, not self-rating | Rozenblit & Keil 2002 (illusion of explanatory depth, strongest for causal knowledge); Bjork, Dunlosky & Kornell 2013 (fluency illusion, desirable difficulties) |
| Retrieval | Blurting, cold recall, review of the gap list | Dunlosky et al. 2013 (practice testing and distributed practice rated high utility); Karpicke & Blunt 2011 (retrieval beat concept mapping with text in view, also on meaningful-learning tests); Blunt & Karpicke (concept maps from memory as effective as free recall) |
| Self-explanation | Why-questions, mechanism probes | Bisra et al. 2018 (meta-analysis, 69 comparisons, g = 0.55; effect mediated by explanation quality; prompts work best when aligned with the learning goal); Rittle-Johnson et al. 2017 (smaller effect in mathematics, about 0.27) |
| Explaining to an audience | Mastery check "explain to a smart beginner" (Feynman) | Fiorella & Mayer 2013, 2014 (learning by teaching; actually explaining matters more than only preparing to teach). The Feynman technique as such is barely studied directly; popular effect sizes are not well grounded. |
| Interactive dialogue | Socratic questioning, one question per turn | Chi & Wylie 2014 (ICAP: interactive > constructive > active > passive) |
| AI tutor design | Withholding answers, hint ladder | Bastani et al. 2025, PNAS (unrestricted GPT-4 improved practice but harmed later unassisted performance; a guardrailed tutor avoided the harm); Kestin et al. 2025, Scientific Reports (a carefully designed AI tutor produced more than double the learning gains of an active-learning class in physics) |
| Productive struggle | Phase A: problem before instruction | Sinha & Kapur 2021 (meta-analysis, 53 studies; better conceptual knowledge and transfer, g about 0.36, no loss in procedural knowledge; requires some prior knowledge) |
| Comparison | Contrasting cases, comparing solution methods | Alfieri, Nokes-Malach & Schunn 2013 (case comparison supports learning; explanation after comparison works better than before); Star, Rittle-Johnson & Durkin 2016 (comparing solution methods in mathematics) |
| Conceptual and procedural knowledge | Quantitative probes linking steps to principles | Rittle-Johnson, Siegler & Alibali 2001; Rittle-Johnson, Schneider & Star 2015 (bidirectional, iterative development; problem representation as a mechanism) |
| Low-utility techniques | Reason the skill avoids summaries and rereading | Dunlosky et al. 2013 (summarization, highlighting, rereading rated low utility) |

Note: the four-part structure for conceptual causality (mechanism, boundary conditions, counterfactual, competing explanations) is a design decision derived from general principles of causal reasoning, not from a specific study.
